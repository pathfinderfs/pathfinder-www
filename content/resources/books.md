These five books have influenced how we think about investing, spending, and what money is for.

<p class="legal-copy">Reading suggestions are general education, not individualized investment, tax, or legal advice.</p>

<div class="book-grid">
  <article class="card book-card">
    <img src="/images/books/your-money-and-your-brain.jpg" alt="Cover of Your Money and Your Brain">
    <h2>Your Money and Your Brain</h2>
    <p class="book-author">Jason Zweig</p>
    <p>Why a market decline can feel like physical danger, why a gain can feel like proof of skill, and why knowing those reactions does not automatically make us immune to them.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="card book-card">
    <img src="/images/books/four-pillars-of-investing.jpg" alt="Cover of The Four Pillars of Investing">
    <h2>The Four Pillars of Investing</h2>
    <p class="book-author">William J. Bernstein</p>
    <p>A rigorous and readable case for diversification, low costs, and humility about anyone's ability to forecast markets.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="card book-card">
    <img src="/images/books/stumbling-on-happiness.jpg" alt="Cover of Stumbling on Happiness">
    <h2>Stumbling on Happiness</h2>
    <p class="book-author">Daniel Gilbert</p>
    <p>Not a finance book. It belongs here because financial plans depend on guesses about what our future selves will value, and people are surprisingly bad at those guesses.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="card book-card">
    <img src="/images/books/your-money-or-your-life.jpg" alt="Cover of Your Money or Your Life">
    <h2>Your Money or Your Life</h2>
    <p class="book-author">Vicki Robin and Joe Dominguez</p>
    <p>A direct examination of what spending costs in time and what “enough” might mean. The questions remain useful even when the specific prescriptions do not fit.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="card book-card">
    <img src="/images/books/seven-stages-of-money-maturity.gif" alt="Cover of The Seven Stages of Money Maturity">
    <h2>The Seven Stages of Money Maturity</h2>
    <p class="book-author">George Kinder</p>
    <p>A way to think about the fears, habits, and aspirations that arrive in the room with the account statements.</p>
  </article>
</div>

Pathfinder does not vote proxies on behalf of clients. A [proxy](https://www.investor.gov/shareholder-voting) lets a shareholder vote on fund or company proposals without attending the shareholder meeting. We can offer general guidance when clients ask; the voting decision remains with the client.

Because of our <a href="/investment-strategy/">investment philosophy</a>, the proxy questions clients are most likely to face involve mutual funds and exchange-traded funds. When clients ask for general guidance, the principle is straightforward: vote in ways that favor lower costs, fewer conflicts, and better long-term shareholder outcomes.

Typical examples include:

- **Advisory contract approval:** generally favor arrangements that support lower fees and disciplined investment management
- **12b-1 fee approval:** generally oppose 12b-1 fees and proposals that create or preserve them. These are [fund fees used for distribution or shareholder services](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/distribution-andor-service-12b-1-fees)
- **Fund merger approval:** generally oppose mergers unless there is a clear shareholder benefit such as lower expenses or a stronger structure
